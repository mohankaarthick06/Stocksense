## T1. Do promotions significantly increase sales?

- **H0:** For the same store x product, median daily units on promo days = on non-promo days
- **H1:** Median daily units are higher on promo days (one-sided)
- **Test:** Wilcoxon signed-rank, paired by store x product (+ per category, Bonferroni)
- **Why this test:** Pairing compares a product with itself, so the mix of which products get promoted cannot fake a lift; daily unit counts are skewed, so a rank test is safer than a t-test.
- **Sample:** 375 store-product pairs
- **Statistic:** W = 68,548
- **p-value:** 6.77e-57
- **Effect size:** median lift +29%; rank-biserial r = 0.94; 93% of pairs gain
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** Promotions raise units sold, and the effect is large (median +29%). Significant in 7/7 categories after Bonferroni: Snacks, Beverages, Household, Frozen, Personal Care, Groceries, Dairy. Strongest: Snacks (+65%).
- **What the manager should do:** Every planned promotion must increase that week's order by the category lift; promotion days are a required forecasting feature.

## T2. Does mean demand differ across store types?

- **H0:** Daily demand per product has the same distribution in Express, Supermarket and Hypermarket stores
- **H1:** At least one store type differs
- **Test:** Kruskal-Wallis + pairwise Mann-Whitney (Bonferroni); robustness: Friedman (product as block), ANOVA on log demand
- **Why this test:** Three groups and skewed demand -> rank test. Restricted to the 48 products sold in ALL formats, so differences in assortment cannot create a false difference.
- **Sample:** 288 store-product combinations (48 products)
- **Statistic:** H = 122.1
- **p-value:** 3.03e-27
- **Effect size:** epsilon^2 = 0.42; median units/day: Express 5.7, Hypermarket 36.6, Supermarket 10.4
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** Store format matters a lot: a Hypermarket sells 6.4x an Express store's volume of the same product. Pairwise (Bonferroni): Express vs Hypermarket p=1.44e-19; Express vs Supermarket p=8.24e-09; Hypermarket vs Supermarket p=7.92e-19. Friedman p = 1.43e-21, ANOVA(log) p = 2.21e-41 - same conclusion.
- **What the manager should do:** Reorder levels and safety stock must be set per store (format), never copied across stores; store_type is a required model feature.

## T3. Is stock-out frequency associated with promotion status?

- **H0:** Stock-out status is independent of promotion status
- **H1:** Stock-out status depends on promotion status
- **Test:** Chi-square test of independence (2x2) + odds ratio, Cramer's V; Mantel-Haenszel by store x category
- **Why this test:** Both variables are binary. With ~70k rows any tiny difference is 'significant', so the odds ratio is what we interpret. Mantel-Haenszel checks that it is not just because promoted products are in stock-out-prone stores/categories.
- **Sample:** 69,554 store-product-days
- **Statistic:** chi2 = 983.1 (df = 1)
- **p-value:** 8.39e-216
- **Effect size:** stock-out rate 13.6% (promo) vs 5.2%; OR = 2.90; Cramer's V = 0.119; MH-adjusted OR = 3.01
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** Promotion days have 2.9x the odds of a stock-out (3.0x after adjusting for store and category, MH p = 5.75e-216). Cramer's V is small because stock-outs are rare overall, but the business effect is large: stores are not ordering for promotions.
- **What the manager should do:** Link the promotions calendar to replenishment: raise stock before a promotion starts; promo_days_next_7 is a key stock-out feature.

## T4. Does temperature drive beverage demand?

- **H0:** No monotonic association between daily temperature and beverage demand (rho = 0)
- **H1:** rho != 0
- **Test:** Spearman rank correlation, within each store (Bonferroni over 6 stores)
- **Why this test:** The relation need not be linear; testing within a store removes store-size differences.
- **Sample:** 1104 store-days
- **Statistic:** median rho = 0.42
- **p-value:** 8.39e-06
- **Effect size:** rho ranges 0.32 to 0.53; significant in 6/6 stores
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** Hotter days sell more beverages in every store (strongest S01, rho = 0.53). p-value shown is the largest (weakest) of the six stores. Temperature also moves with season, so part of this is the summer effect.
- **What the manager should do:** Use the temperature forecast for beverage and frozen orders in summer.

## T5. Is late delivery associated with the supplier?

- **H0:** Late delivery rate is the same for all 10 suppliers
- **H1:** Late delivery rate differs by supplier
- **Test:** Chi-square test of independence (10 x 2) + odds ratio for the worst supplier vs the rest
- **Why this test:** Categorical supplier vs binary late/on-time outcome.
- **Sample:** 10,797 received purchase orders
- **Statistic:** chi2 = 1,078.3 (df = 9)
- **p-value:** 2.28e-226
- **Effect size:** SUP04 late 36% vs 7% others; OR = 7.7; Cramer's V = 0.32
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** SUP04 is the outlier: 7.7x the odds of a late delivery. This explains why its category stocks out most.
- **What the manager should do:** Use SUP04's effective lead time (nominal + average delay) in reorder calculations and raise it with the supplier.

## T6. Do products that ran out in May keep running out after the June reorder recalibration?

- **H0:** Jun-Aug stock-out rate is the same for combinations that stocked out in May and those that did not
- **H1:** It is higher for combinations that stocked out in May (one-sided)
- **Test:** Mann-Whitney U on combination-level Jun-Aug stock-out rates
- **Why this test:** One value per store x product avoids treating 90 correlated days as independent; rates are skewed and many are 0.
- **Sample:** 272 vs 105 combinations
- **Statistic:** U = 19,759
- **p-value:** 2.92e-09
- **Effect size:** mean Jun-Aug stock-out rate 6.0% vs 2.6% (Mar-Apr: 9.0% vs 2.3%); rank-biserial r = 0.38
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** Stock-outs are chronic: the combinations that ran out in May kept running out after the 1 June reset. The reset set levels from May sales, which were capped by empty shelves, so it under-corrected (see EDA chart 8 for the cover in days of sales vs days of estimated demand).
- **What the manager should do:** Recalibrate reorder levels on estimated demand (demand_est), and review every combination that stocked out in May.

## T7. Is weekend demand different from weekday demand?

- **H0:** For the same store x product, median daily units on weekends = on weekdays
- **H1:** They differ (two-sided)
- **Test:** Wilcoxon signed-rank, paired by store x product, overall and per store format (Bonferroni)
- **Why this test:** Paired within product, skewed counts.
- **Sample:** 377 store-product pairs
- **Statistic:** W = 7,115
- **p-value:** 2.48e-41
- **Effect size:** median weekend uplift: Express -6%, Hypermarket +45%, Supermarket +23%
- **Decision:** Reject H0
- **Business interpretation:** The weekend effect is real but its size depends on the format: Express p=2.76e-06; Hypermarket p=7.39e-14; Supermarket p=2.17e-34.
- **What the manager should do:** Deliveries should arrive before the weekend peak in the formats that have one; weekend days in the next 7 is a forecasting feature.
