## How the data was produced

The generator simulates the business process. It does not sample each column independently:

1. **True demand** for each store × product × day. It combines the store's scale and growth, the day of week (each format has its own weekend pattern), promotions (lift varies by category, and long-shelf-life items dip after a promotion), pre-festival buying, holidays, the 1st–5th payday effect, temperature (beverages and ice cream), rain, local events, MRP hikes and the ramp-up of new products. Daily noise is negative binomial.
2. **The company's current reorder rule**: order up to a level once stock plus open orders falls to the reorder level. Planners set reorder levels from a rough baseline that ignores growth, promotions, festivals and heat. **On 1 June they recalibrated the levels using the previous 30 days of *sales***. Because sales are capped by stock, combinations that were running out got reorder levels below their true demand.
3. **Suppliers** deliver after a nominal lead time. Some deliveries are late or partial. SUP04 (all beverages) is late on about 36% of purchase orders.
4. **Sales = min(demand, stock on shelf).** Those units are split into transaction lines with hour, payment mode and customer.
5. **Data-entry noise** is injected afterwards (see "Quality traps").

Transactions reconcile exactly with `inventory.sold_qty` before the noise is added. The generator asserts this.

## Quality traps (all intentional)

| File | Trap |
|---|---|
| transactions | exact duplicate rows · about 1.2% of dates in `DD-MM-YYYY` · IDs in lower case or with stray spaces · `quantity ≤ 0` (returns and voids) · 45 keying errors where quantity was multiplied by 100 (inventory disagrees) · missing `selling_price` · prices about 10× MRP |
| products | category spellings such as `beverage` / `BEVERAGES` / `Beverages␠` / `Grocery` · duplicate rows · one missing `shelf_life_days` · one missing brand |
| stores | `store_type` in mixed case or with stray spaces |
| inventory | `closing ≠ opening + received − sold` (a miscounted closing, or a delivery left unrecorded) · negative closing stock · missing days · duplicate rows |
| external_factors | the city is spelled `Trichy` here but `Tiruchirappalli` in stores.csv · missing temperature/rain · impossible temperatures · duplicate rows |
| (history) | 4 late launches: 2 products on 10 Aug, 2 on 27 Aug (only 5 days of data) |

Large but genuine quantities also exist (bulk buyers, and Chennai hypermarket milk at about 185 units/day). These agree with inventory, so do not cap them blindly.

## Headline properties (seed 2026)

- Share of demand lost to stock-outs: about 6%. Daily stock-out rate is 6.3%. **28% of store × product × day origins have a stock-out in the next 7 days** (29% in the holdout week).
- Stock-out rate is 13.6% on promotion days and 5.2% on other days.
- Beverages stock out on 18% of days, against 3–6% for other categories. The cause is SUP04's late deliveries combined with summer heat.
- S05 (Trichy, under-planned) and S02 (Chennai, fast-growing) stock out most. S03 (Madurai Express) is declining and overstocked.
- After 1 June, combinations that were censored in May were planned at 87% of true demand and kept stocking out at 8.6%, against 3.5% for the rest.
- If no orders are placed on 31 Aug, 65% of combinations stock out during Sep 1–7, losing about ₹11 lakh of revenue.
